How it works

  1. 1

    Pick your area

    Choose a covered district or share your location. Add a field with its crop, growth stage, groundwater and drainage.

  2. 2

    We read NASA data

    Root-zone soil wetness, temperature, rainfall and sunlight from NASA POWER, plus a 7-day forecast from Open-Meteo.

  3. 3

    Rules decide

    Evapotranspiration and a water balance are checked against crop rules. Every threshold is visible in one table.

  4. 4

    You get one answer

    Irrigate, monitor or wait, with a plain-language reason, a salt-risk hint and flush and crop guidance.

IRRIGATEWater deficit is high. Water now.
MONITORClose to the limit. Check the field again soon.
WAITSoil is wet enough or rain is coming. Save water.

What is inside

💧 Irrigation advice

A daily irrigate / monitor / wait signal with the reasons behind it. Logging an irrigation resets the water deficit.

🧂 Salt-risk indicator

Scores conditions that favour salt build-up. If you enter a measured ECe, the measurement replaces the estimate.

🚿 Flush timing

Qualified guidance on when flushing may make sense, based on conditions and your last flush.

🌾 Salt-aware crops

FAO salt-tolerance cards, your priorities (scarce water, preferred crops) and a 3-step rotation idea with reasons.

🗺️ Map dashboard

Pilot districts and your fields coloured by signal. OpenStreetMap, satellite, NASA true-colour and a MODIS vegetation layer.

📅 6-month outlook

A season plan from about two years of past NASA data. It is climatology, not a forecast.

🤖 AI explainer

A small Gemini model explains the rules' result in plain words in your language. It never overrides the rules and answers only from your field's facts.

👥 Farmer community

Share tips by area, like posts, and report bad ones. Three reports hide a post. A verified-agronomist badge is supported.

⚠️ Alerts

Frost and heavy-rain warnings plus the normal advice, delivered in Telegram.

Data and methods

NASA and open data

  • NASA POWER – T2M, T2M_MAX/MIN, PRECTOTCORR, RH2M, ALLSKY_SFC_SW_DWN, GWETROOT, GWETTOP
  • NASA GIBS / MODIS – true-colour and NDVI map layers (visual support only)
  • Open-Meteo – 7-day forecast
  • OpenStreetMap – base map

Methods

  • ET₀ by Hargreaves-Samani (FAO-56 family)
  • Crop salt tolerance: FAO Paper 29, Maas & Hoffman (1977)
  • Every rule lives in one versioned file, labelled “placeholder” until an agronomist reviews it
  • “How was this decided?” shows the exact rule that fired

Built with

  • Python, FastAPI, aiogram
  • Telegram Bot + Mini App (Leaflet)
  • Vercel + Supabase (Postgres)
  • Gemini (lightweight model) for explanations
  • pytest, with a full simulated bot conversation

Built to be honest about its limits

What we do not claim

  • Salt is not measured from space. We score conditions that favour it.
  • NASA POWER's grid is about 50 km, so neighbouring fields look alike.
  • Thresholds are generic and not yet reviewed by an agronomist.
  • No accuracy figure is claimed. This is decision support.

What we checked

We replayed the rules day by day on real NASA data for the driest and wettest 30-day windows in three districts. In 6 of 6 district-seasons the driest window gave at least as many IRRIGATE days and no more WAIT days. Mean soil wetness is ordered IRRIGATE ≤ MONITOR ≤ WAIT.

This is a logic sanity check, not field validation.

Resilience and privacy

  • NASA data is cached and shown as “stale” if NASA is unreachable.
  • Demo and stale data are always labelled.
  • Requests are verified with Telegram's signed login data. You can delete your account and data.
  • The AI gets no names or Telegram IDs.

Questions farmers ask

Do I need to install an app?

No. Sho'r Radar opens inside Telegram, as a bot and a Mini App. You only need Telegram on your phone.

Does it measure the salt in my soil?

No. Salt cannot be seen directly from satellites. We score conditions that favour build-up. If you enter a measured ECe, that measurement decides the salt level instead.

How accurate is it?

We claim no accuracy figure. The rules are generic placeholders until an agronomist reviews them, and we only checked that the signals move in the right direction in dry and wet periods.

Which languages does it speak?

Uzbek, Russian and English, in both the bot and the Mini App. You can switch any time in Settings.

What if NASA data is not available?

The last NASA data is kept and shown, clearly marked as stale. The page also falls back to the last data saved on your phone.

Does the AI decide what I should do?

No. The rules decide. The AI only explains the result in plain words, using the facts about your field, and says so when something is missing.

Is my data safe?

Requests are verified with Telegram's signed login data, and you can only see your own data. You can delete your account and all data in Settings.
